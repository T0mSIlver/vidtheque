Which one / honestly

Both run the same tokens and the same data, so this is a comparison of temperament, not of worlds.

A wins — the results page

Every channel is labelled and none of them fight the image: you can scan eight of these and know which one you want. It is the only treatment that can carry the note: line, the confidence column and the provenance block without looking broken, and the one that still works when the frame is a blue curtain.

The cost: at thumbnail size the detection boxes are decoration — the glow reads, the words inside it do not.

B wins — the distribution loop

The frame is the argument. At 1200 × 630 in somebody's timeline this is the one that stops a scroll, and the OCR box glowing over real slide text is the whole product demonstrated in one image, with no caption needed.

The cost: it drops the apparatus. Confidence, shot window, keyframe id and the note: for a frame with no on-screen text all have nowhere to go, so a spoken-only moment loses most of what makes it trustworthy.

What is real on this page

Frames are the indexer's own keyframes, downscaled to 1120px. Boxes are ocr_lines.x0/y0/x1/y1, normalised 0–1, drawn as percentages — nothing was nudged to fit. Spoken lines are cues.text at the cue that contains the keyframe timestamp, with its neighbours either side. Timecodes are keyframes.t_s truncated to the second, which is exactly what the ?t= parameter takes.

What is not

Speaker names are parsed out of the video title, because speakers is empty in this corpus — no diarisation has run. A production card would either show the diarised speaker or show the channel and say so.
